Why Discipline Is Not the Key to Success

Popular MythSuccess comes from discipline.
Overlooked TruthSuccess comes from devotion.

Luciano Pavarotti has the answer

Pavarotti was once asked by a reporter, “What do you do the night before a big opera? Do you socialize?” Pavarotti replied, “No, I stay home and rest my voice.”

The reporter was impressed. “Wow! You’re so disciplined!”

Pavarotti corrected him. “No,” he said. “It’s not discipline; it’s devotion.”

Discipline is forcing yourself to do something you have to do. It feels like pushing a boulder uphill. Exhausting.

In contrast, devotion is being attracted to what you are meant to do. It feels like you’re finally swimming with the current. Everything flows.

How to win a Nobel Prize

When Richard Feynman was stuck in his physics career, he stopped trying to do “important” work. Instead, he found himself enjoying the way a plate wobbled in the air while he was relaxing in the cafeteria. Feynman thought it would be interesting to calculate its motion.

His colleague asked, “What’s the importance of it?”

“There’s no importance whatsoever!” Feynman replied. “I’m just doing it for the fun of it.”

That playful exploration led to the work that won him a Nobel Prize.

If you feel you need more discipline to be successful in your work, you are doing the wrong kind of work. Instead, choose work that is worthy of your devotion.

Devotion makes discipline unnecessary.

Day 2 of 7

When Does It Make Sense To Start Something New?

Popular MythIf you’ve invested years into a career, it would be foolish to start something new and squander everything you’ve built.
Overlooked TruthNo matter how many years you’ve invested into a career, if it brings you angst, you owe it to yourself to make changes. You deserve peace of mind.

Why do we continue down paths that no longer serve us?

I spent years of my life studying for an Engineering/Law degree. Halfway through, I knew in my gut that I would never become an engineer. But I kept going.

Why? I didn’t want to admit to my friends and family that I had just wasted several years of my life.

This is the sunk cost fallacy: continuing down a path that no longer serves us simply because we’ve already invested time, money, or effort into it.

  • We stay in a job we hate because we’re close to a promotion.
  • We don’t change careers because we have spent so long establishing our skills in our current profession.
  • We cling to a business that drains us because of the years we’ve poured into it.

It’s like refusing to leave a movie you are hating purely because you already paid for the ticket. Never a good idea.

The sunk cost fallacy deceives you into allowing past choices to influence future decisions. See through its evil scheme.

Identify the signs that your current path is no longer serving you.

Identify Your Exit Signal in 3 minutes →

What to do when you get the feeling, “I can’t throw away decades of work!”

Consider Sarah, a corporate lawyer nineteen years into her career. Corner office. Six-figure salary. The respect of her peers.

But every Sunday night, she felt a familiar dread creeping in. She discovered that yoga was the only thing that quieted her anxiety. While her colleagues talked about case law, she daydreamed about teaching wellness.

“I can’t throw away two decades of work,” she told herself. “All that education, all those years would be totally wasted!”

Then she watched a burned-out 62-year-old partner suffer a heart attack and die a week later. The stress and grueling hours came at a price. Another colleague her age was abruptly fired for “not being able to keep up.” Sarah realized that everyone leaves eventually. The only choice is how.

She started teaching yoga on weekends while keeping her law job. She saved her money aggressively. She researched the wellness industry with the same rigor she brought to legal briefs.

Eighteen months later, she opened her own studio.

“People think I threw away my legal career,” Sarah says. “But I didn’t throw it away. Actually, I completed it. Those 19 years taught me discipline, business acumen, and how to serve clients. I use those skills every day. I just use them in work that makes me feel alive.”

Sunk costs are in the past. Focus on what matters to you today.

Day 3 of 7

The Truth About Regret

Popular MythRegret arises from failing to achieve your goals.
Overlooked TruthRegret arises from not acting on the things that deeply matter to you.

The danger of “Now is not the right time”

Yesterday we discussed the sunk cost fallacy. Another sneaky way this fallacy deceives us is by causing us to say, “I know I need to make changes, but now is not the right time.”

A 74-year-old woman once told me, “I spent more than 40 years waiting for the right moment. It never came. I didn’t write the novel. I just kept coming and going to my pointless job.”

When most people get to the end of their lives, few look back to lament their failures. Instead, the regret you’re most likely to have is, “I wish I did the things that I always wanted to do.”

Why don’t we do these things? One of the biggest reasons is that we don’t appreciate just how short life is.

If you live to about 80, you only get about 4,000 weeks. In that time there are only so many:

  • Projects that you can work on.
  • Adventures you will go on.
  • Meals with the people you love.

Life seems infinite. But in reality, it is unforgivingly finite.

In this finite stretch of time you have, there is never going to be a “right time.”

So schedule a time tomorrow and start then.

How to Choose Your Work Today

Popular MythYou must quit your job to choose your work.
Overlooked TruthYou can keep your job and choose your work today.

Michael’s story: From fast-food worker to graphic designer

Michael worked in a fast-food restaurant. He was 26, earning minimum wage, and felt like his creative strengths were going to waste.

So he made a decision.

Each night, he would spend one hour redesigning logos he saw on his commute. No clients. No feedback. Just him, his laptop, and a quiet belief in his ability to design.

Then his car broke down…

The repair wiped out his savings. He had to work extra shifts just to stay afloat. Most people would’ve stopped. Michael didn’t.

He did five-minute sketches during lunch breaks. He recorded short insights on voice notes before bed. It wasn’t perfect — but it was something.

He kept going.

With time and practice, his designs improved. He posted them online. Small businesses started to notice. He got his first few clients. A year later, he left the restaurant for good.

Not because of luck.

Because of one hour a day.

The fastest path to the life you want is a one-hour appointment in your calendar. Schedule it.

How to Overcome the Curse of Competence

Popular MythPursue the things that you’re good at.
Overlooked TruthPursue the things that give you energy.

Jeremy seems like he has it all…

Jeremy is a seasoned graphic designer with a polished portfolio and a steady stream of high-end clients. He works on luxury brand campaigns. His designs are flawless. His calendar is always full. His business is very profitable. On paper, he has reached the pinnacle of his profession.

Alice, on the other hand, is just starting out. She creates illustrations and quirky layouts for small community projects and indie businesses. Her work still has rough edges, but she is utterly fascinated by it. So much so that she finds herself noticing street signs, color combinations in old magazines, and even the way album covers are laid out.

One Friday evening, they sat down after a long day of work.

Jeremy sighed and rubbed his temples.

Alice asked, “Long day?”

“Same as always,” he replied. “Deadlines. Edits. Client politics.”

She paused. “Do you ever enjoy it?”

Jeremy shrugged. “I’m good at it. It pays well. That’s something, right?”

Alice smiled gently. “Yeah, but do you ever feel proud of what you’ve made?”

Jeremy looked away. “Honestly? Not really. I just feel… tired.”

There is a life waiting for you where your work gives you energy instead of draining it from you.

Create Your Energy Ledger in 5 minutes →

Jeremy suffered from the curse of competence.

He did what he was good at, but he was left feeling drained and unfulfilled.

A strength is not accurately defined by what you are good at. Rather, a strength is something that makes you feel stronger. It’s an expression of your true self. A weakness makes you feel weaker, even when you are good at it.

For Alice, graphic design was a strength because it energized her. For Jeremy, graphic design was a weakness because it weakened him.

Jeremy discovered that winning the wrong game only magnifies the inherent emptiness of playing it.

Skills are transferable. Time is not. Now let’s apply your skills to work that energizes you.

Why You Get Fooled by Numbers

Popular MythIf it can’t be quantified, it doesn’t matter.
Overlooked TruthThe most important things in the world cannot be quantified.

How to think independently in a world obsessed with conformity

It’s important to understand the difference between your adapted self and your true self.

Your adapted self conforms to cultural norms. Your true self honors your deepest aspirations.

Your adapted self crafts just the right post for social media. Your true self knows your deepest truth and only wants to express it.

Your adapted self dictates what you should do. Your true self gently encourages you to imagine what you could do.

Your adapted self domesticates you to obtain external validation. Your true self seeks inner well-being.

Your adapted self pursues increased status. Your true self seeks personal growth.

At the end of the day, you need to make an important decision. Decide whether you want to feel happy at your core, or whether you merely want to appear to be happy. Is your appearance more important than your actual experience? If fulfillment is the priority, begin by examining the core of your being and ponder what really matters — what really matters to me? More likely than not, you will discover that the answer does not involve a new pair of expensive shoes or a shiny new car.

Freedom is the ability to direct your energy to the things that matter.

Experience the satisfaction of delivering real value.

Identify Your True Self in 3 Minutes →

The quantification bias

Due to the influence of the adapted self, we are drawn to concrete accomplishments and quantifiable results because these are things that are immediately understood by others.

“Executive Director” comes with clear status. But how you feel after you receive a thank-you card from a client cannot be measured.

Having money in the bank is easily quantified. But the feeling of contentment is immeasurable.

Likes on a post can be counted. But being genuinely seen by another person cannot be quantified.

Quantifiable achievements are very reasonable goals worthy of your effort, if that’s what you desire. But if that’s all you focus on, you will miss out on something far greater. It’s like developing the perfect online presence on social media while neglecting to nurture genuine relationships offline.

It is far more satisfying to create real value than to appear valuable.

The Definition of Success

Popular MythReal success is financial success.
Overlooked TruthReal success is living a meaningful life that fascinates and challenges you.

From the edge of suicide to revolutionary inventor

In 1927, Buckminster Fuller stood at the edge of Lake Michigan, ready to end his life. He was 32, bankrupt, and drinking heavily. His business had failed. His daughter had died. He felt worthless.

But standing there in the dark, something made him pause.

Instead of taking his life, he decided to live as if he had already died. Being dead, he wouldn’t have to worry about himself and would be free to focus his energy on living as a representative of the universe. He decided to gift the rest of his life to one question:

“What is it on this planet that needs doing, that I know something about, that probably won’t happen unless I personally take responsibility for it?”

Buckminster Fuller went from contemplating suicide to inventing the geodesic dome and earning 28 patents. While most people hope to impact one field, Fuller went on to transform three: architecture, engineering, and environmentalism.

The Fuller Question changed my life and it may well do the same for you.

Answer the Fuller Question in 3 Minutes →

Why winning the lottery won’t solve your problems

Despite clear evidence from the likes of Fuller, we in the West have been sold the dream that winning the lottery would allow us to retire on an island and live a life of infinite leisure. The modern version of this dream is to work a four-hour day or even a four-hour week. It’s a clever sleight of hand that tricks us into believing financial abundance can mask a deep-seated void in well-being.

The reality is that tackling problems that matter is a primal need. Contentment is only possible once you have found a challenge that matters to you. It’s no different than any other essential need you have. If you do not exercise for a long time, your muscles begin to atrophy. If you receive endless praise, your capacity to improve is diminished. Growth is impossible in the absence of friction. Unbridled positivity invariably produces stagnation. Ignoring what matters to you feels painless at first. The eventual pain is far greater than any temporary difficulty you’d face from pursuing something meaningful.

Reject the oversimplified versions of success fed to you on social media and define success in your own terms. True wealth cannot accurately be represented by numbers. It is found in doing things purely because they are worth doing. True wealth is the experience of choosing your work.
