From the Edge of Suicide to Revolutionary Inventor
In 1927, Buckminster Fuller stood at the edge of Lake Michigan, ready to end his life. He was 32, bankrupt, and drinking heavily. But standing there in the dark, something made him pause.
He finally decided to live as if he had already died, free to focus his energy on living as a representative of the universe, focused on one question: “What is it on this planet that needs doing, that I know something about, that probably won’t happen unless I personally take responsibility for it?”
Fuller went from the brink of suicide to developing 28 patents, the geodesic dome, and transforming three separate fields: architecture, engineering, and environmentalism.
The Fuller Question changed my life and it may well do the same for you.
